**Eastern Shore High‑School Football Beat – A Practical Guide for the New Reporter**  
*(Business‑Casual, “expert‑to‑rookie” tone)*  

---

## 1.  Why the Eastern Shore is a Unique Beat  

- **Geography & Community** – The “Eastern Shore” of Maryland (Dorchester, Talbot, Caroline, Queen Anne’s, Kent, Somerset & Worcester counties) is a tight‑knit, largely rural area where the high‑school football field is often the social hub of a town. Games draw whole towns, PTA groups, local businesses, and the county’s police & fire departments.  
- **Conference Structure** – Most schools compete in the **Bayside Conference** (Class 1A or 2A depending on enrollment). The conference is split into *East* and *West* divisions, and the regular‑season schedule is heavily driven by geography and historic rivalries.  
- **Season Timeline** –  

| Phase | Approx. Dates | What to Watch For |
|------|----------------|-------------------|
| Pre‑season (training camp, media day) | Early‑July – Mid‑August | New coaches, roster cuts, “next‑man‑up” line‑ups |
| Regular season | Late August – Early November | Weekly game recaps, injury updates, rivalry weeks |
| Playoffs (regional → state) | Mid‑November – Early December | Bracket reseeding, upset potential, “first‑time” match‑ups |
| Off‑season (recruits, coaching moves) | December – June | College commitments, staff turnover, facility upgrades |

Understanding where you are on this calendar will help you plan story angles, deadlines and interview windows.

---

## 2.  Core Workflow (A Quick‑Reference Checklist)

1. **Pre‑Game Prep (Day‑before/Day‑of)**
   - Review the official *Bayside Conference* schedule and any last‑minute changes (e.g., weather postponements).  
   - Pull the latest *team sheet* from the school’s athletic department (roster, depth chart, senior captains).  
   - Check for **injury reports** – most schools issue a brief note after practice; note any “next‑man‑up” story potential.  
   - Set up a **quick interview** request with the head coach (or offensive/defensive coordinator) for a pre‑game quote.  

2. **During the Game**
   - Take **real‑time notes** on scoring drives, turnovers, key performances, and coaching adjustments.  
   - Record **stat lines** for the top five players on each side (TDs, yards, interceptions).  
   - Flag any *controversial calls* or *milestone moments* (e.g., a player surpasses 1,000 rushing yards).  

3. **Post‑Game (Within 2‑4 hrs)**
   - Draft a **90‑word “game recap”** for the next morning’s edition – focus on the final score, decisive plays, and a quote from the winning coach.  
   - File a **longer feature** (500‑800 words) if the game involved a rivalry, a record‑breaking performance, or a coaching change.  
   - Update the **season‑to‑date record** table for each school you cover (keep a master spreadsheet).  

4. **Weekly Wrap‑Up (Friday)**
   - Produce a **“Bayside Round‑Up”** column (≈300 words) summarizing the week’s biggest stories across the conference.  
   - Highlight any *story leads* for the upcoming week (e.g., “Senior QB’s final home game”, “Coach’s first win after a mid‑season firing”).  

5. **Playoff Season (Mid‑Nov onward)**
   - Keep a **bracket tracker** (PDF or spreadsheet) with seedings, reseeding notes, and travel logistics.  
   - Coordinate with **team travel coordinators** for locker‑room access, media passes, and practice schedules.  
   - Prioritize **human‑interest angles** (senior’s last game, a coach’s 20‑year milestone, a town’s “home‑field advantage”).

## 8.  Final Tips for the Eastern Shore Beat  

1. **Be a Community Reporter** – Attend the Friday night pep rallies, meet the boosters, and know the local businesses that sponsor the teams (e.g., “Anderson’s Hardware” or “Keeney’s Deli”). These details make a story feel *homegrown*.  
2. **Leverage Social Media** – Most schools post game‑day photos on Facebook or Instagram. Screenshot a standout moment and add a quick “stat‑line” caption – it drives engagement.  
3. **Respect Small‑Town Sensitivities** – A senior’s final game can be emotional. Phrase “last‑play” stories with respect (“senior’s final home‑field touchdown”).  
4. **Cross‑Check Records** – The Bayside Conference website posts official standings each week; use it to verify win‑loss totals before publishing.  
5. **Build a “Season‑Long” Narrative** – Keep a running list of “season‑defining moments” (e.g., a coach’s 100th win, a player’s 1,000‑yard season). At season’s end, you’ll have a ready‑made feature.
