### 1. How the Beat Fits the Paper  

The Star‑Democrat’s readership lives in the Eastern Shore counties and follows the Bayside Conference with the same intensity as the local fishing season.  Football is the paper’s biggest high‑school sport – the games generate the most page‑turns, the most letters to the editor, and the most community‑event advertising.  Your job is to turn the on‑field drama into a story that tells the **who, what, why and how** of each program while keeping the focus on the *programs* – coaches, culture and rivalries – rather than on any single player.

### Colonel Richardson High  

Colonel Richardson has been a **run‑first, ball‑control** team for the past few seasons.  Coach James Jackson repeatedly preaches “run the football, keep the ball in our possession as long as possible, wearing defenses down.”  The Colonels have turned that philosophy into results – a 55‑0 shutout of Lancaster (Oct. 4 2024) and a 56‑0 win over the same opponent a month later.  The program has **no state titles** in the recent era, but the 2024 season shows a clear upward trend: outscoring opponents 203‑20 over the last four games (Oct. 29 2024).  

**Identity & Style** – Ground‑and‑pound attack that emphasizes hitting the holes, a “run the ball every play” mantra, and a defense that prides itself on speed and tackling.  

**Quick‑Reference**  

- **Head Coach:** James Jackson (cited in every Colonel article).  
- **Key Contact (Quotes):** “We’ve been preaching all week, the backs hitting the holes.” – Jackson, Oct. 8 2024.  
- **Rivalries (mentioned):** Kent County (multiple regular‑season games), North Caroline (Oct. 9 2024), Cambridge‑South Dorchester (2024 Class 1A playoffs).

### Cambridge‑South Dorchester High  

The Vikings have become the **story of the season**.  Rob Burnham took over in July 2024 and, in his first year, has steered the team to an unbeaten regular season (10‑0) – the first time the program has reached a 5‑0 record since 2000.  Burnham’s philosophy is blunt: “run the ball every play, run it effectively at every turn.”  The offense is built around senior quarterback Anthony Hughes, who combines passing with a strong rushing attack, and junior running back Sterling Perkins, who has already eclipsed the 1,000‑yard mark.  Defensively, Burnham boasts “the backbone of our team” – a fast, aggressive unit that has posted three shutouts in the past five games.  

**Identity & Style** – Run‑heavy offense anchored by Hughes and Perkins; a defense that values speed, aggressive tackling and varied blitzes.  

**Quick‑Reference**  

- **Head Coach:** Rob Burnham (first‑year head coach, July 2024).  
- **Key Assistants:** Former assistant Donny Graef (offensive line), defensive coordinator (not named in the archive).  
- **Rivalries (mentioned):** Kent County (multiple regular‑season games), Colonel Richardson (2024 Class 1A playoffs).

### Kent County High  

Kent County’s identity is **versatile**.  Under first‑year head coach Zach Davis (appointed Aug. 27 2024), the Bucs have run a mixture of “zone, power, counter and buck‑sweep” schemes while also deploying a spread package when needed.  The 2024 season turned the program around dramatically: after a 1‑4 start, the Bucs went 5‑0 (Oct. 4 2024) and later beat Cambridge‑South Dorchester 46‑8, giving the Vikings their first 8‑0 start since 2000.  The team’s senior quarterback Gavan Henry is a dual‑threat who throws and runs, while running backs Jaxson Hess and Nate Green provide a potent ground game.  

**Identity & Style** – Balanced attack with zone‑run concepts, power and counter formations, plus a spread passing element; emphasis on “run zone, power, counter, buck‑sweep.”  

**Quick‑Reference**  

- **Head Coach:** Zach Davis (10th season as a head coach, first at Kent County).  
- **Key Player (coach quote):** “We’re a blue‑collar, old‑school football team.” – Davis, Oct. 8 2024.  
- **Rivalries (mentioned):** Queen Anne’s County (homecoming, Oct. 8 2024), Colonel Richardson (regular season), Easton (Class 2A playoffs, Nov. 15 2024).

### Stephen Decatur High  

Decatur is the **benchmark of success** on the Eastern Shore.  Head coach Jake Coleman has guided the Seahawks to **back‑to‑back Class 2A state championships** (2023 and 2024) – the first time a Bayside program has repeated titles since the early 1990s.  The 2024 team finished 14‑0, beating Westlake 27‑20 in overtime to reach the state final and then winning a 13‑12 thriller over Huntingtown on a last‑minute 32‑yard touchdown pass to Zakhari Baker.  Coleman describes the squad as a “power football” team that runs a heavy ground game (senior RB Landon Cawley: 670 yds, 13 TDs) and employs a dual‑threat quarterback (Johnny Hobgood, 1,973 yd passing, 32 TDs, 2 INT).  Defensively, the Seahawks field a “very athletic” unit that has recorded five shutouts this season.  

**Identity & Style** – Power‑run offense with a dual‑threat quarterback; a stout, athletic defense that mixes aggressive blitzes with solid coverage.  

**Quick‑Reference**  

- **Head Coach:** Jake Coleman (multiple articles, 2023‑2024).  
- **Key Contacts:** Senior QB Johnny Hobgood (lead passer), AD not reported.  
- **Rivalries (mentioned):** Huntingtown (state title rematch 2023 & 2024), Westlake (state semifinal 2024).

### Queen Anne’s County High  

Brian Mooney, now in his second year, runs a **hybrid offense** that mixes a modern spread attack with the “double‑wing” concepts of former head coach Donny Graef, who now serves as offensive‑line coach.  The Lions have shown grit, pulling off a 20‑15 win over James M. Bennett (Oct. 1 2024) and a 45‑0 shutout of Kent County (Oct. 13 2024).  Defensively, coordinator Jude Mandes mixes varied coverages and blitz packages, allowing the Lions to hold opponents to a season‑low six points against North Caroline (Oct. 8 2024).  

**Identity & Style** – Offensive blend of spread and double‑wing; aggressive defense with mixed coverages and blitzes.  

**Quick‑Reference**  

- **Head Coach:** Brian Mooney (since 2023).  
- **Key Assistants:** Donny Graef (offensive line), Jude Mandes (defensive coordinator).  
- **Rivalries (mentioned):** Kent County (homecoming, Oct. 8 2024), James M. Bennett (Oct. 1 2024), North Caroline (Oct. 8 2024).

### Easton High  

Matt Griffith’s Warriors have become the **underdog story** of the season.  Ranked 10th in a preseason poll, the team finished 8‑4, captured two Class 2A East‑Region playoff wins and recorded its first home playoff victory since 1993 (62‑36 over Parkside, Nov. 8 2024).  Senior QB Mikey O’Connor is a classic dual‑threat: 1,938 yd passing, 21 TDs, plus 1,436 yd rushing and 19 TDs.  The offense leans heavily on the run – O’Connor’s “run the ball every play” mantra – while also using a balanced aerial attack to keep defenses honest.  Defensively, the unit is built around the “ball‑hawk” play of safety Zach Barnhart (multiple interceptions) and the tackling prowess of senior linebacker Jordan Horsey.  

**Identity & Style** – Dual‑threat quarterback, run‑first philosophy, emphasis on ball control and limiting turnovers.  

**Quick‑Reference**  

- **Head Coach:** Matt Griffith (10th year).  
- **Key Contacts:** Senior QB Mikey O’Connor (team leader).  
- **Rivalries (mentioned):** Kent Island (multiple playoff meetings), Queen Anne’s County (Oct. 18 2024), Stephen Decatur (potential future matchup).

### Kent Island High  

Zach Davis took the helm in 2024, inheriting a program that has **re‑engineered its identity** around a mixed “zone, power, counter, buck‑sweep” run scheme plus a spread passing package.  The Buccaneers posted a 5‑0 streak (Oct. 4 2024) and later beat the 9‑0 Cambridge‑South Dorchester Vikings 42‑0 (Oct. 25 2024), snapping a 24‑year drought of an unbeaten start.  Senior QB Gavan Henry (38 carries, 142 yd, 7 TDs) and senior RB Jaxson Hess (123 yd, 9 TDs) are the primary weapons.  The defense is described as “very athletic” and capable of forcing turnovers, as evidenced by four takeaways in the win over Colonel Richardson (Oct. 4 2024).  

**Identity & Style** – Run‑centric attack with zone‑run, power, counter and spread concepts; a fast, opportunistic defense.  

**Quick‑Reference**

- **Rivalries (mentioned):** Queen Anne’s County (homecoming, Oct. 8 2024), Kent County (regular season), Easton (Class 2A playoffs, Nov. 15 2024).

## 3. Practical Reporting Workflow  

Below is a **check‑list** you can keep on your phone or notebook for each game you cover.  The items are ordered chronologically for a typical game day.

1. **Pre‑Game Prep (24‑48 hrs before)**
   - Verify the **official start time and venue** (e.g., “Andy Schipul Stadium, 6 p.m.”).  
   - Pull the **quick‑reference box** for both schools (head coach, key contacts, rivalries).  
   - Scan the recent **press releases** for any coaching quotes or injury updates.  
   - Review the **last‑5‑games trend** for each program (e.g., “Colonel’s 203‑20 run differential over last four opponents”).  

2. **Morning of the Game**
   - Call the **head coach’s office** (use the listed name; if a phone number is not in the archive, request it from the school’s athletic director).  
   - Confirm **weather** (several games have been affected by wind and rain – e.g., Decatur’s overtime win in “steady, wind‑driven rain”).  
   - Get a **media credential** from the school’s AD or the conference office.  

3. **During the Game**
   - Note **key play‑calling themes**:  
     * Run‑first emphasis (Colonel, Kent County, Decatur).  
     * Hybrid spread/double‑wing (Queen Anne’s).  
     * Balanced zone‑run/power (Cambridge‑South Dorchester).  
   - Track **turnovers** – many articles highlight their impact (e.g., Easton’s 4 turnovers vs Queen Anne’s).  
   - Record **coach sound‑bites** that echo their season‑long philosophy (e.g., “run the ball every play” – Burnham).  

4. **Post‑Game (within 1 hr)**
   - Secure **quotes** from the head coach and any assistant who is on the field.  
   - Verify **statistical highlights** (e.g., O’Connor’s 250‑yard rushing game vs Kent Island, Decatur’s 13‑12 overtime win).  
   - Update the **season narrative**: Are we seeing a resurgence, a repeat champion, a program in decline?  

5. **Follow‑Up (next day)**
   - File a **story outline** that places the game in the broader context of the program’s history (e.g., “first home playoff win since 1993” for Easton).  
   - Cross‑check any **player milestones** with the school’s record books before publishing (e.g., Bradley Thomas’s 75‑yard TD – first Colonels TD of that length in recent memory).

## 4. Tips for Staying on Target  

- **Stay program‑centric:** Mention the **coach’s name** early, then describe the **team culture** and **season arc**.  
- **Use the archive’s exact phrasing** when you can.  Readers recognize a coach’s own words (“We’ve been preaching all week, the backs hitting the holes,” Jackson).  
- **Avoid speculation:** If a play style is not described, write “N/A” rather than guessing.  
- **Tie each game to a larger story:** For example, Kent County’s 5‑0 run after a 1‑4 start is a “turn‑around narrative” that the paper’s audience finds compelling.  
- **Remember the audience:** Many families in the Eastern Shore read the paper for *community* news.  Highlight the **local flavor** – homecoming crowds, senior night ceremonies, and the impact of a player’s college commitment (e.g., Zymear Smith’s verbal commitment to Maryland).
