**The Eastern Shore High‑School Football Beat – A Guide for New Reporters**  
*Star‑Democrat, Easton, MD*  

---

### 1.  Why This Beat Matters  

The Eastern Shore of Maryland is a tight‑knit community where high‑school football is a social cornerstone.  Families, alumni, local businesses and even the county government turn to the *Star‑Democrat* for the stories that define the season – from a new coach’s first‑year philosophy to a small‑town program’s surprise run to a state title.  Your reporting will shape how readers understand each program’s identity, its continuity (or change) in the coaching staff, and the rivalries that fire up the Bayside Conference each Friday night.

---

### 2.  The Landscape – Conferences and Classifications  

- **Bayside Conference** – The core of our coverage.  Most schools compete in Class 1A (the smaller schools) or Class 2A (the larger schools).  The conference feeds directly into the state playoffs, so a game that looks “just another regular‑season matchup” may be a quarter‑final or a title‑deciding contest.  

- **Class 1A Powerhouses** – *Fort Hill* (three‑time defending 1A champion) and *Cambridge‑South Dorchester* (unbeaten 2024, first 9‑0 season since 2000).  

- **Class 2A Leaders** – *Stephen Decatur* (back‑to‑back state champion, 12‑0 regular season in 2024) and *Milford Mill* (recent regional winner).  

Understanding where a school sits in this hierarchy will help you decide whether a story is a “building‑program” piece, a “playoff‑preview” or a “legacy‑recap.”

### 3.  Program Profiles – Narrative Summaries  

Below is a short narrative for each school that falls within the *Star‑Democrat*’s coverage area.  Each paragraph pulls directly from the reporting we have already published, so you can trust every fact.

---

#### Cambridge‑South Dorchester High (The Vikings)  

The Vikings entered 2024 with a historic push.  After a 9‑0 start – the first time the program has been unbeaten that deep since 2000 – they have now posted a 10‑0 record, the best in school history, and earned the No. 1 overall seed in the Class 1A playoffs.  The turnaround began with the promotion of **Rob Burnham** to head coach in July.  Burnham, a 25‑year veteran who spent the previous four seasons as offensive line coach under former head coach **Toby Peer**, says his philosophy has not changed: “I’m competitive to a fault… I will lose sleep to prepare for a game.”  He now runs both the offensive and defensive lines while delegating the offense to a staff that includes former head coach **Donny Graef** (offensive‑line coach).  

The Vikings have built their identity around a bruising ground game – junior running back **Sterling Perkins** has eclipsed 1,000 rushing yards, and senior quarterback **Anthony Hughes** adds over 700 yards on the ground.  Defensively, the unit is led by seniors **KaMarii Stanley**, **Christen Dockins**, **Antonio Blueford** and **Tabrion Watts**, each with 40+ tackles, and the team has recorded three shutouts in its last five outings.  

*Rivalries*: No long‑standing rivalry is documented in our sources; the Vikings meet a variety of opponents each week, including Century, Kent County, Colonel Richardson and Washington.  

---

#### Kent County High (The Trojans)  

The Trojans have struggled in 2024, opening the season 0‑5 before a late‑season surge that included a 46‑8 win over Cambridge‑South Dorchester on October 4.  Their head coach is **Zach Davis**, a first‑year leader who succeeded **Bryon Sofinowski** after his retirement.  Davis emphasizes a “hard‑nosed, old‑school” style: “We like to run the football, try to keep the ball in our possession as long as possible.”  The Trojans’ offense is anchored by senior running backs **Jaxson Hess** (≈1,000 yards) and **Nate Green**.  

*Rivalries*: None documented in our sources.  

---

#### Colonel Richardson High (The Colonels)  

Colonel Richardson has re‑emerged as a force in 2024, posting a 5‑3 record after a three‑game win streak that included a 56‑0 homecoming shutout of Lancaster.  **James Jackson** is the head coach; he praises the “gritty” nature of his squad and notes a steady improvement in the offensive line and running game.  Defensive coordinator **Tim Goodrich** returns after a three‑year stint and has helped the team hold opponents to a season‑low 6 points in the recent shutout of Lancaster.  

Key contributors on the ground include senior running back **Derrick Mitchell**, who rushed for 100+ yards in the Lancaster game, and senior wide receiver **Ja’Zir Dickerson**, who also contributed as a rusher.  

*Rivalries*: None documented.  

---

#### Queen Anne’s County High (The Lions)  

The Lions have been a story of resilience.  After losing their starting right tackle, left tackle, quarterback and several starters on the line early in the season, head coach **Brian Mooney** kept the program “gritty.”  Mooney, now in his second year, runs a spread offense blended with the double‑wing concepts of former head coach **Donny Graef**, who returned as offensive‑line coach.  The Lions have rallied to a 4‑2 record, highlighted by a 20‑15 win over James M. Bennett and a 45‑0 shutout of Kent County.  

Senior running back **John Connolly** (over 100 yards per game) and sophomore quarterback **Carter Hercher** (converted from defensive back) embody the program’s “next‑man‑up” culture.  

*Rivalries*: A homecoming loss to **Easton** (27‑26) on October 18; a regular‑season win over **James M. Bennett** (20‑15) on October 1.  

---

#### Easton High (The Warriors)  

Easton has been the surprise “underdog” story of the Eastern Shore.  Ranked No. 10 in a preseason poll, the Warriors finished the regular season 8‑3, won two playoff games—including the program’s first home playoff victory since 1993 (a 62‑36 win over Parkside) – and entered the state quarterfinals 8‑3.  **Matt Griffith**, now in his tenth year, stresses a balanced attack: “We have a good core that we can cycle through and get breaks on offense, and not have any drop‑offs.”  Senior quarterback **Mikey O’Connor** has become the centerpiece, posting 250 rushing yards and three TDs in a single game against Kent Island, while also throwing for 146 yards.  The offensive line, rebuilt after losing four starters, has found stability under the guidance of graduate assistants and senior linemen.  

The program’s identity is built on “believing in themselves,” a theme the coaching staff has repeated after early‑season setbacks and a 1‑8 record the year before.  

*Rivalries*: Long‑standing rivalry with **Kent Island** (multiple meetings each season, including a 28‑21 playoff win on November 15), and a heated matchup with **Queen Anne’s** (27‑26 win on October 18).  

---

#### Kent Island High (The Buccaneers)  

The Buccaneers returned to the postseason after a 1‑9 season in 2023, posting a 7‑3 record in 2024 under first‑year head coach **Zach Davis**.  Davis describes his team as “hard‑nosed” and “blue‑collar,” emphasizing a run‑first approach led by senior running backs **Jaxson Hess** and **Nate Green**.  The Buccaneers opened the 2024 season with a 9‑3 loss to Stephen Decatur but have since strung together a four‑game winning streak, highlighted by a 21‑20 home win over **Queen Anne’s** on October 29.  

*Rivalries*: None documented.  

---

#### North Caroline High (The Bulldogs)  

North Caroline’s 2024 season began with a 9‑2 record and a playoff run, but a mid‑season coaching change has left the program in flux.  **Nic McMorris** was fired after a 0‑5 start, and **Heath Wilkins**, previously the junior‑ varsity head coach, was promoted to varsity head coach.  The Bulldogs still have senior talent at running back **Zymear Smith**, who has verbally committed to the University of Maryland.  

*Rivalries*: None documented.  

---

#### Stephen Decatur High (The Seahawks)  

The Seahawks are the benchmark of success on the Eastern Shore.  Under **Jake Coleman**, they captured back‑to‑back Class 2A state titles in 2023 and 2024, finishing the 2024 regular season 12‑0 and entering the state playoffs undefeated.  Coleman, a former player on two of the program’s earlier title runs, relies on a balanced attack led by junior quarterback **Johnny Hobgood** (1,973 passing yards, 32 TDs) and a deep receiving corps that includes **Davin Chandler** (Vanderbilt commit) and **Trybe Wise** (Georgetown commit).  The defense has been “elite,” posting five shutouts this season.  

*Rivalries*: None documented in our sources.  

---

#### Fort Hill High (The Sentinels)  

Fort Hill entered 2024 as the three‑time defending Class 1A champion, having won 21 straight games before falling to Cambridge‑South Dorchester in the quarterfinals.  Head coach **Zack Alkire** describes the team as “ground‑and‑pound” with a senior‑heavy roster.  Star senior running back **Jabril Daniels** (≈1,600 rushing yards, 26 TDs) continues to be the focal point of the offense.  The Sentinels have a reputation for physical, fast football and have produced several Division I recruits.  

*Rivalries*: None documented.  

---

#### James M. Bennett High (The Clippers)  

The Clippers have struggled in recent years, but a 37‑0 win over Easton in 2024 gave them a brief flash of competitiveness.  Head coach **Brendan Riley** praised senior wide receiver **Zakai Smullen**, calling him “the most under‑recruited player in the Bayside Conference.”  The program is looking to build around that talent as the season progresses.  

*Rivalries*: None documented.  

---

#### Parkside High (The Rams)  

Parkside’s most notable moment this season was an upset of Easton (37‑30) on October 11, powered by running back **Karontez Bratten** (four rushing TDs).  Head coach **Brendan Riley** (also coaching the Clippers) highlighted the team’s “resilient” character.  

*Rivalries*: None documented.  

---

#### Westlake High (The Wolverines)  

Westlake earned a semifinal berth in the Class 2A playoffs, beating Stephen Decatur in 2023 and now looking to repeat.  Senior running back **Devin Cox** (1,554 rushing yards, 29 TDs) and junior quarterback **William Drakeford Jr.** lead the offense.  No coaching changes are noted in the source material.  

*Rivalries*: None documented.  

---

#### Huntingtown High (The Hurricanes)  

Huntingtown reached the 2024 Class 2A state championship game, falling to Stephen Decatur 13‑12 in overtime.  Senior running back **Landon Cawley** (670 yards, 13 TDs) and junior quarterback **Dominic Hickman** (920 passing yards) were key contributors.  No coaching details are supplied.  

*Rivalries*: None documented.

### 5.  Reporting Workflow – From Pre‑Game to Publication  

1. **Pre‑Game Prep (2 days before)**  
   - Review the latest game recap for each team to spot trends (e.g., a new offensive scheme, injury updates, coaching quotes).  
   - Update the “program identity” paragraph for the school: is it a “run‑first, gritty” team, a “balanced, high‑scoring” squad, or a “defensive stalwart”?  
   - Verify the head coach’s name and any recent staff changes (e.g., a new offensive coordinator).  

2. **Game Day**  
   - Arrive at the stadium at least 30 minutes early to capture locker‑room atmosphere and pre‑game remarks.  
   - Record at least two sound bites from the head coach (or assistant) – one about the game plan, one reflecting on the program’s broader narrative.  
   - Take photos of the team’s logo, the crowd, and any distinctive traditions (e.g., Easton’s “home‑playoff” celebration).  

3. **Post‑Game (within 1 hour)**  
   - Draft a 300‑word “quick‑look” recap for the website, emphasizing the program’s identity and any coaching continuity or change highlighted in the game.  
   - For playoff or rivalry games, add a 600‑word feature that weaves in historical context from past seasons (e.g., Easton’s first home‑playoff win since 1993).  

4. **Weekly Wrap‑Up (Monday)**  
   - Compile a one‑page “Bayside Pulse” that lists each school’s current record, next opponent, and any notable story angles (e.g., a senior’s final season, a coach’s first year).  
   - Flag any emerging story leads for deeper pieces (e.g., the impact of a new head coach at Cambridge‑South Dorchester, or the rise of a small‑town program to a state title).  

5. **Fact‑Checking**  
   - All statistics (yardage, touchdowns, win‑loss records) must be cross‑checked against the official box score posted by the school’s athletic department.  
   - Direct quotes must be verified from the audio recording or written notes; do not paraphrase in a way that changes the speaker’s intent.

### 6.  Angles That Resonate With Our Readers  

- **Coaching Continuity vs. Turnover** – How a school’s identity survives (or evolves) when a new head coach steps in (e.g., Rob Burnham at Cambridge‑South Dorchester).  
- **Program Identity** – The “gritty, hard‑nosed” culture of Kent Island, the “balanced, high‑scoring” approach of Stephen Decatur, or the “defensive dominance” of Fort Hill.  
- **Rivalry Stories** – When a long‑standing rivalry produces a dramatic finish (Easton vs. Kent Island, Easton vs. Queen Anne’s).  
- **Historical Context** – Connecting a current season to a milestone (e.g., Easton’s first home playoff win since 1993, Cambridge‑South Dorchester’s first unbeaten run since 2000).  
- **Player‑to‑Coach Perspectives** – How senior leaders (e.g., Mikey O’Connor at Easton) embody a program’s values, without turning the story into a player‑profile piece.  

---

### 7.  Final Thought  

Your role on the beat is to turn the Friday night lights into a narrative that the Eastern Shore community can see itself in – a story of coaches who build cultures, of schools that rise and fall, and of rivalries that make a small town feel like a championship arena.  Keep the focus on the programs, the people who run them, and the history that gives each game its weight.  When you do, the *Star‑Democrat* will remain the go‑to source for every Eastern Shore football fan.
