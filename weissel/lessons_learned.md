Reflection from taking JOUR329W in the Fall of 2025. 

I recall speaking to my mother about using an electric typewriter to write her essays in College. What a novel idea. I'm not sure if I have ever seen an electric typewriter in my entire life. 

This is a pretty much how I feel about the state of LLM's right now. In 20 years I invision telling my kids that I used Claude, ChatGPT and Groq models in a course and they look at me the same way I looked at my mom when she told me that. 

LLM's are so freaking cool in December of 2025, but the same way my mom thought her electric typewriter was cool 40 years ago. It's not that the technology isn't facinating and cutting edge, but it doesn't take a genius to know that AI is going to get so so so much better. 

I came into this course only having used LLM's for headline writing and grammar fixes in jouanlsim context. This course showed me that LLM's are necessarly tools to succeed in the journalism world going foward. I honestly feel that strongly becasue of how easy it was to use CoPilot to asssit me with my coding work. 

The biggest struggles with using LLM's in this course is that they mess things up a lot. Whether that is making up detials or disregarding necessary context or not following a provided script LLM's are nowhere near perfect. 

Someone using LLM's has to know this and dillegently fact check their work at every step. This was something I did not do well enough and led me to have to do a lot of extra work (some of which I am still doing) to ensure my final Beat Book is accurate. 

It is a dilemma in journalism, where truth matters so much but LLM's will allow us to write stories that have never been done before. Specifically with local sports coverage I invision a reporter setting up an LLM to get only the stat sheet from a game that the journalist did not going to becasue there are many high school games at a time and write the "5 things to know from Friday's win." 

At the point we are at right a sinnister view of using LLM's could be "truth seekers use lying machines to write stories." In some ways from this semester I agree with this but that is why I would advise people using LLM's to be extremely careful with instrcutions and fact checking before publishing LLM assisted content. The way LLM's speak about their work and their process comes across as 100% blind confidence. I have got reponses like this before: "I read the story to ensure you have the best and most accurate results." And then the LLM spits back some BS. 

LLM's are cool, fun, fascinating...all the buzz words. But you have to be educated on how they work before diving in and asking them to do something. 

I would also tell students working with news archives and stories to really understadn your material first before ever feeding it to an LLM. You have to know what you want to get out of the prompt and request so having background knowledge and ideas already in your mind is helpful. I found that having and LLM prompt another LLM to get a result is cool but usally misses the mark on exactly what I wanted. 

Think about it like going to the grocery store, you shouldn't stand in the checkout line with nothing in your cart. In other words don't prompt and LLM until you know what you want out of it. 

From these past few months I have continued to increase my LLM usage in my personal life too, there are just some things it is not worth struggling to figure out on my own when an LLM can do the trick. Yes I get annoyed when they don't help me but LLM's are only going to get better so I will continue to use them as we both evolve.
