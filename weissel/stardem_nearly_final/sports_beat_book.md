### Overall Tips for Covering High School Sports on the Eastern Shore  
1. **Know the geography & rivalries** – Map the Bayside Conference (North & South), ESIAC, and county‑line match‑ups (e.g., Easton vs Kent Island, Cambridge‑South Dorchester vs Colonel Richardson). Travel times often add 15‑30 min; use them for “road‑trip” angles.  
2. **Embed in the community** – Attend booster meetings, summer town‑team games, and off‑season clinics. The same family names appear on rosters, PTA minutes, and local business sponsorships. Trust = deeper story leads.  
3. **Master the calendar** – Keep a color‑coded master schedule for every sport. Spring overlaps (baseball, softball, lacrosse) and fall clashes (football, field hockey, soccer) require daily prioritization.  
4. **Use an “Impact‑Decision Matrix”** – When games conflict, rank by rivalry intensity, playoff stakes, milestone potential (coach’s 200th win, player records), and editor requests. Cover one live; file the other via phone/stats.  
5. **Build a stats & contacts database** – Log season stats, injury updates, roster changes, and coach/AD cell numbers in a shared sheet. Update after every game; it becomes your competitive edge.  
6. **Recap formula** – Lead with the decisive moment (walk‑off hit, overtime goal, shutout), insert a coach/player quote within the first three paragraphs, then key stats. Aim 300‑500 words for print; expand for web with play‑by‑play.  
7. **Look beyond the score** – Angle ideas: senior profiles, coaching milestones, Title IX growth (girls’ wrestling, lacrosse), weather‑affected fields, recruiting/college commitments, and injury comebacks.  
8. **Respect eligibility & safety** – Verify names with the AD before publishing. Concussion or injury stories require parent/AD clearance; handle them sensitively.  
9. **Snap quick action photos** – If a photographer isn’t assigned, take a few phone shots of key plays. Action images boost social‑media clicks.  
10. **File fast, fact‑check faster** – Friday night games need Saturday morning copy. Draft the lede in‑game; confirm final stats with the official scorer or head coach before filing.  
11. **Balance gender coverage** – Track story counts by sport and gender each quarter. Give equal weight to softball, field hockey, girls’ lacrosse, etc.  
12. **Stay visible & accessible** – Introduce yourself to every coach at season start, share your cell, and return calls/texts within two hours—even on weekends.

## Baseball  
- **Scoring & key stats** – Runs, hits, errors (RHE line). Track ERA, batting average, RBI, and pitch counts (MPSSAA limits: 110 pitches/day, mandatory rest).  
- **Decisive factors** – Pitching depth (starters rarely beyond 5 innings), timely hitting with runners in scoring position, and late‑inning defensive errors.  
- **Terminology** – “Wheels came off” (bullpen collapse), “dinger” (HR), “can of corn” (easy fly), “small ball” (bunts/steals), “QAB” (quality at‑bat).  
- **What to watch** – Pitch‑count proximity to limits, base‑running miscues, momentum swings after errors, and any “manufactured” runs (no hits).  
- **Story angles** – Pitching duels/shutouts, walk‑offs in rivalry games, senior college‑bound arms, weather‑forced doubleheaders, breakout performances from under‑the‑radar players.  
- **Interview tips** – Pitchers: “What was working today?” Hitters: “What did you see on that pitch?” Coaches: ask about pitch‑count strategy, injury updates, and bench decisions.

## Basketball  
- **Scoring & key stats** – Points, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks; shooting percentages (FG %, 3P %, FT %). Double‑double & triple‑double are instant hooks.  
- **Decisive factors** – Free‑throw shooting in tight games, defensive intensity in the fourth quarter, and halftime adjustments. Watch foul trouble (5 fouls = out).  
- **Terminology** – “And‑one,” “downtown” (3‑pointer), “coast‑to‑coast,” “in the paint,” “transition defense.”  
- **What to watch** – Tempo (fast‑break vs. half‑court), rebound battles, scoring runs (e.g., “12‑0 run to close the third”), and disparity in FT attempts.  
- **Story angles** – Coach milestone wins, buzzer‑beaters, family legacies on the roster, transfer‑student impacts, playoff‑seed implications.  
- **Interview tips** – Pinpoint the game’s turning point. For losses ask, “What would you do differently?” For blowouts, shift to player development or upcoming challenges.

## Field Hockey  
- **Scoring & key stats** – Goals, assists, goalkeeper saves, penalty corners (PCs) awarded & converted, shots on goal. Track PC‑battery conversion rate.  
- **Decisive factors** – PC execution (≈50 % of goals), midfield possession, and goalkeeper performance. “Clog the circle” defenses can neutralize strong offenses.  
- **Terminology** – PC, “green card” (warning), “yellow card” (temporary suspension), “stroke” (penalty shot), “drag‑flick.”  
- **What to watch** – Midfield control, PC count per side, goalkeeper workload, late‑game substitution of an extra attacker.  
- **Story angles** – Senior keeper heroics, PC‑specialist profiles, undefeated streaks, state tournament runs, club‑team pipelines feeding rosters.  
- **Interview tips** – Ask PC specialists about technique, goalies about positioning, coaches about halftime adjustments and whether they “played their game” or adapted.

## Football  
- **Scoring & key stats** – TDs, FG, PAT (1‑ or 2‑pt), turnovers, total yards, 3rd‑down conversions, time of possession. QB stats (C/Att, Yds, TD, INT) dominate recaps.  
- **Decisive factors** – Turnovers, red‑zone efficiency, special‑teams (punts, kick returns, blocked kicks). Momentum swings after a “three‑and‑out” or long scoring drive.  
- **Terminology** – “Three‑and‑out,” “red zone,” “sack,” “pick‑six,” “winning the trenches.”  
- **What to watch** – Injuries (especially QBs, O‑line), weather impact (wind, rain), clock control in 4th quarter, fourth‑down gamble outcomes.  
- **Story angles** – Rivalry recaps, playoff‑seed scenarios, coaching milestones, QB duels (often siblings/cousins), injury comebacks, homecoming color pieces.  
- **Interview tips** – Query coaches on key play‑calls, players on assignments for decisive plays, and always ask “what went right” before probing mistakes.

## Lacrosse  
- **Scoring & key stats** – Goals, assists, saves, ground balls, face‑off win % (boys), caused turnovers, man‑up goals.  
- **Decisive factors** – Face‑off dominance (extra possessions), goalie save % (≥ 60 % strong), clean clears without turnovers.  
- **Terminology** – “Man‑up/man‑down,” “clear,” “ride,” “EMO” (extra‑man offense), “crease” (goal circle).  
- **What to watch** – Face‑off specialist match‑ups, number of shots faced per goalie, conversion of man‑up chances, late‑game ground‑ball battles.  
- **Story angles** – Breakout attackmen/midfielders, goalie duels, sibling rivalries, playoff runs, Title IX growth in girls’ lacrosse.  
- **Interview tips** – Ask face‑off guys about technique, goalies about reading shooters, coaches about adjustments to “ride” or “clear” strategies.
