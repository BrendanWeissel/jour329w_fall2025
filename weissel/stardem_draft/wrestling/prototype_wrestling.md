# Eastern Shore High‑School & Community Sports Beat – Wrestling Guide  
*For the reporter who’s just been handed the wrestling beat and wants to hit the ground (and the mat) running.*

---

## 1.  Local Sports Landscape Overview  

### What matters most on the Eastern Shore?  

| Sport | Why it’s a “big deal” | Core audience |
|------|----------------------|----------------|
| **Wrestling** (both boys and girls) |  • Long‑standing rivalries – Bayside Conference, 2A‑1A East Region, and the MPSSAA state tournament.<br>• Rapid growth of girls’ programs (first state team title in 2024, a historic moment for the region).<br>• Small‑town pride: every town’s “Seahawk”, “Lions”, “Buccaneers”, “Warriors”, “Colonels” etc. are instantly recognizable to readers. | Parents, students, booster clubs, local officials, and the handful of regional newspapers that cover high‑school sports. |
| Football | Season dominates the fall calendar; drives attendance and ad revenue for the papers. | Same as wrestling, plus broader community (businesses, alumni). |
| Basketball | Winter spotlight; crossover fans with wrestling because many wrestlers also play hoops. | Same as wrestling. |
| Baseball/Softball | Summer “off‑season” filler; often the only sport on the news desk in July‑August. | Families looking for end‑of‑school‑year stories. |

> **Bottom line:** On the Eastern Shore wrestling is the second‑most‑read high‑school sport (after football) and the only one that now commands equal interest for boys *and* girls. Anything you write about the sport will find a built‑in audience.

### Seasonal Rhythm & Story Opportunities  

| Time of Year | Typical Activity | Story Hooks |
|--------------|------------------|-------------|
| **August – September** | Pre‑season conditioning, weight‑class cuts, summer tournaments (e.g., Iron Duals, Maverick Duals). | “What the wrestlers learned in summer”, “Freshman weight‑class shake‑ups”, “Coaches’ off‑season expectations”. |
| **October – November** | Dual‑meet season kicks off.  Early‑season conference matchups (Bayside, Southern, etc.). | “Rivalry preview: Decatur vs. Kent Island”, “First‑year wrestlers making an impact”. |
| **December** | Conference championships (Bayside, Southern, etc.) – often rescheduled for weather. | “Championship preview”, “Impact of rain‑outs on seeding”. |
| **January – February** | Regionals (2A‑1A East).  Mid‑season “dual meets” with non‑conference schools. | “Regional preview”, “Weight‑class hot‑shots to watch”. |
| **March** | State tournament (Show Place Arena, Upper Marlboro).  The only week the whole Eastern Shore is on a statewide stage. | “State‑tournament storylines”, “First‑time state champions”, “Girls’ historic title”. |
| **April – May** | Off‑season, wrestling club clinics, recruiting (college scouts), “what‑next” for seniors. | “Senior exit interviews”, “College‑bound wrestlers”, “Growth of girls wrestling”. |
| **June – July** | Summer camps, community tournaments (e.g., community recreation league, youth club). | “Youth pipeline”, “Community impact of wrestling”. |

*Tip:* Keep a master calendar (Google Calendar or an Airtable view) that flags **every dual‑meet, conference tournament, regional, and state date** for every school on the Eastern Shore. When a date moves (rain‑out, venue shift), you’ll see the ripple effect across your coverage plan instantly.

## 2.  Reporter Lifestyle & Workflow  

### A Typical Week (Monday‑Friday)

| Day | Core Tasks | Time‑Block Tips |
|-----|------------|-----------------|
| **Monday** | Review weekend results, file quick recap (150‑300 words). Update your stats spreadsheet. | **Morning:** Pull the official score sheets (often PDFs from the school website). <br>**Mid‑day:** Draft and send recap to editor (deadline ≈ noon). |
| **Tuesday** | Prep for mid‑week duals – call coaches for line‑ups, confirm weight classes, verify venue. | **Morning:** 15‑minute phone call + email confirmation. <br>**Afternoon:** Update your “match‑prep sheet” (opponent’s season record, key wrestlers). |
| **Wednesday** | Attend the dual (or two if you can juggle a short drive). Take notes, photos, and record short audio quotes. | **Early‑am:** Arrive 30 min before start for warm‑up observations. <br>**During:** Use a one‑page “Mat‑Log” (see Section 5). |
| **Thursday** | Write the full‑blown game recap (600‑900 words) + a “quick‑look” graphic (team scores, pins, majors). | **Morning:** First draft. <br>**Late‑am:** Send to copy‑edit, embed graphics, upload photos. |
| **Friday** | Preview next weekend’s marquee matchup. Interview a coach/athlete for a human‑interest angle. | **Morning:** Research opponent stats. <br>**Afternoon:** Record interview, craft 400‑word preview. |
| **Weekend (optional)** | Cover a community tournament or youth clinic. | If you have a “day off” assignment, treat it like a regular dual: arrive early, take photos, capture the vibe. |

**Late‑Night Games:**  
- Many conference championships run 10 a.m. – 5 p.m.; the final bouts can stretch into the evening. If you’re on a night‑shift schedule, block out a “post‑game debrief” hour (30 min) to transcribe notes before they blur.

**Long Travel Distances:**  
- The Eastern Shore’s geography means a 45‑minute drive can be the norm, a 90‑minute drive (e.g., Easton → Princess Anne) the exception. Keep a **fuel‑and‑snack kit** in the car; a coffee stop doubles as a quick “coach check‑in”.

**Quick Turnaround Deadlines:**  
- Most editors want the recap **within 4 hours** of the final whistle for the online edition. Draft on a tablet, use voice‑to‑text for quotes, and have a pre‑written boilerplate (score line, venue, date) ready to paste.

### Contact‑List Management  

1. **Master Sheet** – a Google Sheet with columns: Name, Role (coach/AD/parent/booster), School, Phone, Email, Preferred contact method, Last contact date.  
2. **Tagging System** – add tags like “girls‑coach”, “Bayside‑gatekeeper”, “injury‑source”. Use the filter to pull up the exact group you need.  
3. **Quarterly “Refresh”** – before each new season (July), send a brief “intro” email to every contact confirming details. Update any changes immediately after a game (e.g., a new assistant coach appears).  

### Balancing Multiple Beats  

- **Beat‑stacking**: During the dual‑meet season, you may also be assigned basketball or football. Use **shared logistics** (e.g., travel to the same school) to file both stories in one trip.  
- **Overlapping Playoffs**: When the wrestling regionals clash with football playoffs, prioritize *deadline‑driven* stories (state tournament recaps go live first). Use **short “flash” updates** (tweet‑style or a 150‑word “quick‑note”) for the secondary beat until you can write a full piece.

## 3.  Building & Maintaining Relationships  

### First‑Time Introductions  

| Who | Approach | Sample Opening Line |
|-----|----------|---------------------|
| **Head Coach** | Phone call (or in‑person at a pre‑season meeting). Bring a notepad, not a recorder. | “Hi Coach August, I’m [Your Name] with the Star Democrat. I’d love to sit down before the season opens to hear your thoughts on this year’s roster.” |
| **Athletic Director (AD)** | Email with a clear subject: “Eastern Shore Wrestling Beat – Intro & Request for Season Calendar”. | “Good morning Mr. Sitar, I’m covering high‑school wrestling for the paper and would appreciate a copy of the district’s official schedule and any media‑access guidelines.” |
| **Parents/Boosters** | Attend the first **pre‑season meeting** (often held at the school gym). Offer to bring a “season‑preview” article that highlights the booster club’s contributions. | “I’m writing a feature on how community support fuels our wrestlers; could I interview a booster who’s been involved since the program’s inception?” |
| **Players** | After a match, ask for a quick 2‑minute “post‑match sound‑bite” while the locker room is still buzzing. Keep it informal. | “Congrats on that pin, Jake—what’s your mindset going into the next weight class?” |

### Consistent Follow‑Up (Without Being Intrusive)  

1. **After‑Game Thank‑You** – a brief text or email: “Great match today, Coach Waters. That 100‑lb pin was a highlight. I’ll have the recap up by 2 p.m.”  
2. **Monthly Check‑In** – a 5‑minute call to the **gatekeeper** (team manager or veteran booster) to confirm roster changes, upcoming events, or any media requests.  
3. **Birthday/Season Milestones** – send a quick “Happy birthday, senior captain!” note on the athlete’s birthday; it’s a small gesture that builds goodwill.

### Identifying Gate‑Keepers  

- **Team Manager** (often a senior or a parent) – controls the **official roster sheet** and can confirm who is eligible for a match.  
- **Long‑time Volunteer** – the person who runs the **post‑match snack table**; they usually know the locker‑room politics and who’s approachable.  
- **Booster Club President** – can provide **financial context** (funding for travel, equipment) and often has the most polished **photo archives**.

### Roster Management (Names, Spellings, Changes)  

- **Live Spreadsheet**: When you first meet a wrestler, type their name exactly as they spell it (verify with a roster sheet or a photo of a jersey).  
- **Duplicate‑Check**: Use Excel’s “Conditional Formatting → Duplicate Values” to flag potential misspellings (e.g., “Keenan” vs. “Keenan”).  
- **Season‑End Audit**: At the end of each season, cross‑reference your list with the school’s published year‑end roster to capture transfers, graduations, and new freshmen.

### Handling Sensitive Conversations  

| Situation | Best Practice |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Injury** | Quote the coach or athletic trainer *only* after they’ve confirmed the injury is public. Avoid speculation (“He’ll be out for weeks”) unless you have a direct statement. |
| **Academic Ineligibility** | Treat it like a *private* matter. If a coach mentions a wrestler is “not eligible,” simply note that the athlete “did not compete” without elaborating on grades. |
| **Transfer** | Verify with both schools’ ADs. Use neutral language: “Junior Jordan Cox transferred from [School A] to [School B] ahead of the season.” |
| **Disciplinary Issues** | Give the school a **right‑to‑reply** deadline (usually 24 hours). If the school declines to comment, note that “the school declined to comment.” |

## 4.  Reporting & Writing Guidance  

### Story Types & When to Use Them  

| Story Type | Core Elements | Typical Length | When to Pitch |
|------------|---------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Game Recap** | Final score, point‑by‑point breakdown, standout performers, key moments, quick coach quote, stats box. | 600‑900 words | After every dual (or at least the marquee ones). |
| **Advance Preview** | Opponent’s record, head‑to‑head history, key match‑ups, coach’s expectations, any storyline (e.g., “first meeting of two senior seniors”). | 350‑500 words | 1‑2 days before a major dual or tournament. |
| **Feature** | Human‑interest angle (e.g., a senior’s last season, a family tradition, a coach’s 20‑year tenure), background research, multiple quotes, vivid scene‑setting. | 1,000‑1,500 words | Weekly or monthly, especially around milestones (state title, historic first). |
| **Trend Piece** | Data‑driven analysis (e.g., “Boys’ pin‑rate has risen 12 % over the past 3 seasons”), charts, expert commentary. | 800‑1,200 words | Mid‑season when you have enough data points. |
| **Accountability / Investigative** | Examination of a controversy (e.g., weight‑cutting practices, booster influence on team selections). Requires multiple sources, documents, and often a “right‑to‑reply.” | 1,200‑2,000 words | When a story has public impact; always run by editor first. |
| **Profile** | Deep dive on a single athlete, coach, or administrator; life outside the mat, motivations, future plans. | 1,200‑1,500 words | When an athlete lands a historic win or a coach reaches a milestone (e.g., 20th year). |

#### Anatomy of a Strong Recap (Example Layout)

1. **Headline** – Include the final score and a hook: “Decatur 44, Kent Island 23 – Seahawks Keep Bayside Streak Alive”.  
2. **Lede (1‑2 sentences)** – State the result, venue, and the most dramatic moment.  
3. **Body Paragraphs** –  
   - *First paragraph*: Quick narrative of the turning point (e.g., a five‑pin streak).  
   - *Second paragraph*: Highlight a standout wrestler (pin, major decision).  
   - *Third paragraph*: Coach’s perspective (quote).  
   - *Fourth paragraph*: Brief statistical snapshot (team points, pins, majors).  
4. **Bottom Line** – What the result means for the upcoming schedule (e.g., “The win puts Decatur at 9‑1 overall and secures the top seed in the Bayside tournament”).  
5. **Stat Box** – Table of weight‑class results (winner, method, time).  

**Writing Tips**  

- **Active voice**: “Waters **pinned** Tate in 4:21,” not “A pin was recorded by Waters.”  
- **Specificity beats generic adjectives**: “He wrestled a **technical fall** 16‑0” vs. “He won big.”  
- **Avoid “wrestling‑speak” for the general reader**: Explain “major decision” (win by 8‑14 points) in a parenthetical if you think readers might not know.  

### Maintaining Fairness & Steering Clear of Boosterism  

- **Balance quotes**: For every booster quote you include, pair it with a coach or opponent perspective.  
- **Fact‑check every statistic** (pin counts, win‑loss records) against two sources: the school’s official sheet and the conference’s posted results.  
- **Declare conflicts of interest** if a coach you’ve interviewed is a longtime friend or former teammate (a brief note in your pitch is enough).

## 5.  Stats, Rosters, and Data Management  

### Collecting & Verifying Stats  

1. **Primary source** – The official **dual‑meet score sheet** (often a PDF emailed by the school’s AD).  
2. **Secondary source** – The **conference website** (Bayside, Southern) which posts a compiled results page.  
3. **Tertiary source** – **Live‑tweet feeds** from the school’s athletics department; useful for real‑time corrections.  

**Verification workflow:**  

- Pull the PDF → copy into a spreadsheet row → cross‑check each weight‑class result with the conference page → flag any discrepancies in red.  
- If a discrepancy remains, call the team manager for clarification.  

### Building Your Personal Database  

- **Tool**: Airtable or a Google Sheet with separate tables for *Rosters*, *Schedules*, *Results*, *Individual Stats*.  
- **Key fields**:  
  - Wrestler ID (unique code: school‑weight‑year, e.g., “QA‑100‑23”).  
  - Name, class (Fr., So., Jr., Sr.), weight class, season win‑loss, pins, majors, injuries.  
  - “Season‑High” (best finish at regionals/state).  
- **Automation tip**: Use Zapier to import any new PDF posted to a school’s website into your sheet automatically (set up a “new file in folder → parse PDF → append row”).  

### Staying Organized Across Seasons  

- **Seasonal folders**: Create a master folder named “2024‑25 Wrestling” → sub‑folders for each school (e.g., “Queen Anne’s County”). Inside each school folder, keep: *roster.csv*, *schedule.pdf*, *photos/*, *interviews/*, *notes.txt*.  
- **Version control**: When the roster changes (e.g., a sophomore moves up a weight class), **duplicate** the previous roster and rename it with the date of change (e.g., “Roster‑2024‑01‑15”). This creates a paper trail and prevents “who‑was‑where” confusion later.  

### Tracking Unofficial Record Books & Rivalry Histories  

- **Rivalry log** – A simple table: *School A*, *School B*, *All‑time dual‑meet record*, *Notable moments* (e.g., “First girls dual‑meet in MPSSAA history, 2024”). Update after each meeting.  
- **Playoff trends** – Use a pivot table to count how many times a school has reached the top‑four in the last five years. This yields ready‑made “trend” story angles.

## 6.  Playoffs, Overlaps & Coverage Strategy  

### Decision‑Making Framework for Competing Events  

| Factor | Weight (1‑5) | How to Apply |
|--------|-------------|--------------|
| **Impact on standings** (e.g., conference title on the line) | 5 | Prioritize any match that decides a championship or seeding. |
| **Story uniqueness** (first ever girls dual, historic rivalry) | 4 | Even a lower‑stakes match can be a must‑cover if it’s a “first”. |
| **Logistics** (travel time, venue capacity) | 3 | If two games are simultaneous and 90 mi apart, choose the one with higher readership. |
| **Player spotlight** (senior’s last match, a state qualifier) | 4 | Feature a senior’s final dual if you can get a quote. |
| **Editor’s directive** | 5 | Always follow the editor’s top‑priority list. |

Calculate a quick score (sum of weighted factors) for each potential game; the highest score gets your on‑site coverage.  

### Staying Ahead of Scheduling Changes  

- **Subscribe to the conference’s mailing list** (Bayside, Southern). They send out “schedule change alerts” the moment a rainout is logged.  
- **Create a “contingency sheet”**: list each upcoming dual with a secondary opponent (if the primary is cancelled). Many schools already have a “backup opponent” ready for conference play.  
- **Use a shared Google Calendar** with color‑coded events: *Green* = confirmed, *Yellow* = pending weather, *Red* = postponed. The editor and any other beat reporters can see it instantly.

## 7.  Wrestling‑Specific Section  

### 7.1  Unique Rhythms of the Wrestling Season  

| Phase | What Happens | Typical Angles |
|-------|--------------|----------------|
| **Pre‑Season (Aug‑Sept)** | Weight‑class cuts, summer camps, “Iron Duals”. | “How wrestlers manage weight safely”, “What the summer camp taught the team”. |
| **Early Dual Season (Oct‑Nov)** | Conference battles intensify; first major rivalries surface. | “Decatur’s unbeaten run”, “Bayside’s 61‑match streak”. |
| **Mid‑Season (Dec)** | Conference tournaments (Bayside, Southern) – often the first chance to see **girls’ duals**. | “Girls’ historic dual at MPSSAA”, “Coaches weigh the cost of forfeit vs. full line‑up”. |
| **Regionals (Jan‑Feb)** | 2A‑1A East Region – a “gateway” to state. | “Regional preview: top five weight classes to watch”. |
| **State (Mar)** | Three‑day tournament; statewide exposure. | “First‑ever girls state team title for Queen Anne’s”, “Senior senior showdown”. |
| **Post‑Season (Apr‑Jun)** | Off‑season training, youth clinics, college recruiting. | “Senior seniors eye Division I scholarships”, “Community impact of youth wrestling”. |

### 7.2  Typical Storylines on the Eastern Shore  

1. **First‑ever milestones** – First girls dual, first team state title, first senior pin in the final seconds.  
2. **Weight‑class battles** – “Two‑year rivalry at 115 lb: Conard vs. Davis”.  
3. **Freshman phenoms** – Freshmen who win a pin or a major decision (e.g., Finley Patchett).  
4. **Coaching legacies** – Families with multiple generations on the mat (e.g., “Bree Conard, granddaughter of Hall‑of‑Famer Dan Zottarelli”).  
5. **Cross‑sport athletes** – Wrestlers who also star on the football team (e.g., “Defensive player of the year in football, now a state finalist in wrestling”).

| Position / Role | Angle Ideas |
|----------------|-------------|

### 7.4  What to Watch for During Games  

| What to Observe | Why It Matters | Quick Note‑Taking Tips |
|----------------|----------------|------------------------|
| **Schemes & Tendencies** | Some teams favor “double‑leg attacks” while others rely on “switches”. Spot patterns for a trend piece. | Write a two‑column list: *Opponent* – *Preferred move* (e.g., “Decatur – Switches”). |
| **Momentum Swings** | A string of pins can demoralize the opponent; note the point where the lead ballooned. | Mark the **pin streak** in your Mat‑Log with a star. |
| **Key Stats** – Pins, major decisions, technical falls, **takedown percentages** (often posted on the scoreboard). | Provides the numbers for your recap’s “Stat Box”. | Capture the exact **time** of each pin (e.g., “1:20 in 1st period”). |
| **Athlete Body Language** – confidence vs. frustration; helps with vivid descriptions. | Adds color to your feature. | Jot a one‑sentence “Observation” after each bout (e.g., “Waters stared intently, tapping his foot, before the pin”). |
| **Referee Interactions** – controversial calls, “no‑call” moments. | Possible accountability story. | Record the **referee’s name** (often posted on the mat) and the call. |

### 7.6  Sources to Cultivate  

| Source | What They Provide | How to Build Rapport |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|
| **Head Coach** | Strategic vision, team culture, official stats. | Meet before the season, attend a practice, ask about coaching philosophy. |
| **Assistant / Position Coach** (e.g., “girls’ head coach John Waters”) | Technical insight, wrestler development. | Request a short interview on “pinning fundamentals” and use their sound‑bite in a feature. |
| **Team Manager** | Roster updates, travel logistics, last‑minute changes. | Offer to spotlight them in a “Behind the Scenes” short piece. |
| **Parents / Booster Club Leaders** | Funding stories, community impact, historical context. | Attend booster meetings, thank them publicly in articles. |
| **Referees** | Perspective on rule interpretations, controversial calls. | Ask for a quick interview after a match; they appreciate acknowledgment of their role. |
| **College Recruiters** (if relevant) | Insight on athlete pathways, scholarship trends. | Reach out after the state tournament; they love quoting future‑prospects. |

### 7.7  Typical Controversies & Recurring Issues  

1. **Weight‑cutting & Health** – Wrestlers sometimes drop 10‑15 lb rapidly. Watch for any **coach comments** about “making weight” that could be interpreted as unsafe.  
2. **Booster Influence** – Occasionally a booster may try to “push” a particular athlete for a lineup spot. Keep an eye on **unusual roster changes** right before a big meet.  
3. **Eligibility & Transfer Rules** – The MPSSAA has strict transfer rules; a sudden appearance of a senior from another school can trigger questions.  
4. **Girls’ Program Funding** – Historically, girls’ wrestling receives less equipment and travel money. A story on **budget disparities** can be a timely accountability piece.

## 8.  Types of Wrestling Stories – Structure & How‑to  

### 8.1  Game Recap (600‑900 words)  

1. **Headline** – Score + Hook (e.g., “Decatur 44, Kent Island 23 – Seahawks Keep Bayside Streak Alive”).  
2. **Lede** – One sentence: result + most dramatic moment (pin, upset).  
3. **Narrative Flow** –  
   - *Early lead*: “Decatur opened with a 12‑0 surge after a quick 100‑lb pin by Hinojosa.”  
   - *Turning point*: “A five‑pin run by Kent Island in the second period swung momentum.”  
   - *Key performers*: brief bullet‑style stats for top 3 wrestlers on each side.  
4. **Quote Block** – Coach’s short comment (2‑3 lines).  
5. **Stat Box** – Table of each weight class (winner, method, time).  
6. **Implication** – What the win/loss means for the next schedule or conference standing.  

### 8.2  Advance Preview (350‑500 words)  

1. **Headline** – “Bayside Showdown: Decatur vs. Kent Island – Who Will Break the Streak?”  
2. **Hook** – “Both teams enter the mat with 9‑1 records and a combined 15 pins in the last two weeks.”  
3. **Head‑to‑Head History** – 2‑3 sentences summarizing past meetings.  
4. **Key Match‑ups** – List weight‑class battles to watch (e.g., “115‑lb: Conard (QA) vs. Davis (PKS) – both seniors with 4 pins each”).  
5. **Coach Insight** – One quote from each coach on preparation.  
6. **Prediction** – Your own “take” (e.g., “If Decatur can dominate the middleweights, they’ll likely win”).  
7. **Logistics** – Time, venue, ticket price (if applicable).  

### 8.3  Feature (1,000‑1,500 words)  

1. **Narrative Lead** – Paint a scene (e.g., a senior lacing up headgear for the last time).  
2. **Background** – Athlete’s family history, school’s wrestling culture, community support.  
3. **Multiple Voices** – Coach, parent, teammate, and perhaps a **college recruiter**.  
4. **Conflict & Resolution** – A challenge (injury, weight‑cut issue) and how the athlete overcame it.  
5. **Wider Relevance** – Tie the personal story to a larger trend (e.g., growth of girls’ wrestling in Maryland).  
6. **Conclusion** – Forward‑looking statement (senior’s future, program’s next steps).  

### 8.4  Trend Piece (800‑1,200 words)  

- **Data Hook** – “Pins per dual have risen 15 % over the past three seasons in the Bayside Conference.”  
- **Methodology** – Briefly explain how you compiled the data (official score sheets, conference logs).  
- **Expert Commentary** – Quote a coach or a sports analyst.  
- **Implications** – What does the trend mean for training, safety, or recruiting?  

### 8.5  Accountability / Investigative (1,200‑2,000 words)  

- **Lead** – State the allegation (e.g., “Parents allege unsafe weight‑cutting practices at XYZ High”).  
- **Evidence** – Documents, interviews, public records.  
- **Multiple Perspectives** – Coach, athletic director, parent, medical professional.  
- **Conclusion** – Summary of findings, any corrective actions taken, and the next steps.

## 9.  What to Avoid  

### Common New‑Reporter Mistakes  

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | Fix |
|---------|--------------|-----|
| **Relying on a single source** (e.g., only the head coach) | Leads to bias, missing context, and potential inaccuracy. | Always get at least two independent quotes (coach + player, or booster + manager). |
| **Boosterism** – over‑praising a team because boosters fund your newspaper. | Undermines credibility; readers notice unbalanced coverage. | Keep a mental “balance score” – for every booster quote, include a neutral or opposing perspective. |
| **Clichéd phrasing** (“took the mat by storm”, “wrestled his heart out”). | Makes the piece feel generic and lazy. | Replace with concrete images: “His hand gripped the mat, fingers digging into the worn‑out vinyl as he forced a reversal.” |
| **Vague descriptions of moves** (“a good takedown”). | Readers unfamiliar with wrestling are left confused. | Briefly define the move or give a visual: “a double‑leg takedown, where the wrestler drives both legs into the opponent’s thighs and lifts.” |
| **Missing the “why”** – focusing only on the “what happened”. | No story depth; no audience connection. | Ask “Why does this pin matter?” – is it a momentum shift, a record‑breaking moment, or a comeback? |
| **Over‑using the same structure** (always “score → quote → stats”). | Readers get bored; story feels formulaic. | Mix it up: start with a vivid anecdote, then embed the numbers later. |

### Ethical Traps  

- **Favoritism** – If you have a personal connection (e.g., you wrestled in high school), disclose it to the editor.  
- **Using Unverified Stats** – Never publish a win‑loss record that you haven’t double‑checked; a simple typo can cause a school to look incompetent.  
- **Pressuring Athletes** – Avoid asking a senior to comment on a loss immediately after the match; give them a few minutes to breathe, then approach.  
- **Exploiting Injuries** – If a wrestler is injured, keep the description factual, avoid dramatizing (“crushed his spine”) unless you have a medical professional’s confirmation.  

### Avoiding Clichés & Repetitive Gamer Structures  

- **Swap “Pin” for “Fall”** only when you want variety, but keep the terminology consistent within a single piece.  
- **Don’t start every paragraph with “The match…”** – vary sentence openings: “A sudden‑victory period saw…”, “Coach August shouted…”, “The crowd rose to a roar when…”.  
- **Use vivid verbs**: “ripped”, “snapped”, “executed”, “countered”, “weathered”.
