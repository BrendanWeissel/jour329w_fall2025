`````markdown
## Eastern Shore Baseball Beat Guide

```markdown
*Business‑casual – a senior reporter’s play‑by‑play for the newcomer*  

---  

### 1. Local Sports Landscape Overview  

The Eastern Shore of Maryland is a tight‑knit patchwork of small towns, each with a high‑school that serves as a community hub. Baseball is the beating heart of that patchwork for three reasons:  

1. **Tradition** – Almost every public high school fields a varsity baseball team; the sport has been a Friday‑night staple since the 1960s. Generations of families sit in the same bleachers, and alumni boosters still drive the fund‑raisers that keep the fields lit.  
2. **Rivalries** – The **North Bayside Conference** (St. Michael’s, Colonel Richardson, Easton, etc.) and the **South‑East Division** (Decatur, Stephen Decatur, Cambridge‑South Dorchester) produce built‑in story angles each season. A 3‑run walk‑off in a rivalry game instantly becomes “the talk of the county.”  
3. **Participation** – From freshman “fresh‑men” leagues to summer “town‑team” adult leagues, the region’s baseball participation rate dwarfs that of soccer or lacrosse. That means a constant flow of names, stats, and human‑interest angles.  

**Seasonal Rhythm** –  

| Time of Year | Typical Calendar | Story Opportunities |
|--------------|------------------|---------------------|
| **Late February – Early March** | Pre‑season tryouts, first “C‑team” games, weather‑delayed practice | Player‑profile of “late‑bloomer” athletes, feature on weather‑impact on field maintenance |
| **Mid‑March – Early May** | Regular‑season conference play (≈ 20‑30 games per team) | Game recaps, rivalry previews, trend pieces on pitching arms, “coach’s corner” weekly column |
| **Mid‑May – Early June** | Playoffs, district tournaments, state series | High‑stakes game coverage, accountability stories on eligibility, human‑interest on seniors’ last games |
| **June – August** | Summer leagues, community tournaments, field‑renovation projects | Off‑season features, “where are they now” updates, community‑impact pieces |

Understanding where you are in that cycle tells you what type of story the audience expects on any given day.

---  

### 2. Reporter Lifestyle & Workflow  

**Weekly Rhythm** (sample Monday‑Sunday schedule)  

| Day | Core Tasks | Notes |
|-----|------------|-------|
| **Monday** | Review weekend game notes, pull stats from the previous week, update your personal database | Early start (7 a.m.) helps you beat the deadline for Monday’s print deadline (12 p.m.). |
| **Tuesday** | Pitch a preview for Wednesday’s marquee rivalry; confirm travel plans & field access | Keep a shared Google Sheet with travel distances; the Eastern Shore’s schools are often 30‑45 minutes apart, but traffic around the bridge can add 15 minutes. |
| **Wednesday** | Attend the rivalry game (often a night game), take live notes, snap quick action shots (or coordinate with the school photographer) | Bring a compact voice recorder; a 30‑second “sound‑bite” from a coach right after the game is gold. |
| **Thursday** | Write the game recap (300‑500 words for print, longer for web); file the story in the CMS by 4 p.m. | Use the “5‑W‑1‑H + Quote + Stat” template (see Section 4). |
| **Friday** | Follow‑up interviews (injury updates, player reactions), update roster changes, scout the next opponent’s box score | Keep an “open‑questions” list that you can email to coaches over the weekend. |
| **Saturday** | Attend a non‑baseball community event (e.g., town‑team tournament) to stay visible; file a short “community spotlight” piece | This builds goodwill with the boosters and volunteers who run those events. |
| **Sunday** | Light admin: clean up the database, confirm next week’s schedule, recharge (the beat is a marathon, not a sprint). | |

**Travel & Turn‑around Tips**  

* **Map‑It‑First** – Plot all games for the week on a single map; plan a “hub‑and‑spoke” route to minimize back‑tracking.  
* **Pack Light, Pack Smart** – A small notebook, a tablet with a stylus (for quick diagramming of plays), a spare battery pack, and a weather‑proof recorder.  
* **Late‑Night Games** – Use the downtime to draft the recap outline on your phone; you’ll only need to fill in the final stats when they’re posted.  

**Balancing Multiple Beats** (e.g., baseball + softball during spring)  

* Create a **master master calendar** that lists *all* sports events. Color‑code baseball in navy, softball in pink.  
* When two games overlap, apply the **Impact‑Decision Matrix** (see Section 6) to decide which game merits live coverage and which can be covered via a delayed recap.  
* Keep separate, but linked, contact lists for each sport—most coaches sit on both benches, but the booster clubs differ.

---

**Final Thought:**
Baseball on the Eastern Shore is more than a sport; it’s a cultural thread that ties families, schools, and towns together. Your job as the beat reporter is to illuminate that thread—through crisp recaps, thoughtful features, and data‑driven insights—while staying fair, accurate, and deeply embedded in the community you serve. Play hard, write harder, and enjoy the crack of the bat.

## 1. Local Sports Landscape Overview

````markdown
**High‑School & Community Basketball Beat – Eastern Shore (Maryland)  
A Business‑Casual Playbook for New Reporters**

---


The Eastern Shore’s high‑school basketball scene is a micro‑cosm of the region’s identity.  The sport lives at the intersection of three forces that shape every story you’ll file:  

* **Community Tradition** – Small towns (Easton, Cambridge‑South Dorchester, Queen Anne’s, St. Michael’s, Wicomico, Kent Island, etc.) treat basketball games like Saturday night church socials.  Generations of families have sat in the bleachers, and the same surnames (Hollis, Tucker, Kern, Dantzler) appear on rosters and in the booster‑club minutes year after year.  When a coach hits a milestone or a freshman posts a double‑double, the narrative isn’t just “a good game”; it’s “a new chapter in a town‑wide story.”

* **Rivalries & Conference Politics** – The **Bayside Conference (North & South)** and the **Eastern Shore Independent Athletic Conference (ESIAC)** provide built‑in drama.  Easton vs Cambridge‑South Dorchester, Queen Anne’s vs Easton, and the decade‑long “Warriors‑Lions” rivalry generate heightened interest, ticket sales, and, occasionally, heated confrontations (the recent forfeit‑and‑no‑spectator incidents are a case in point).  Knowing which rivalry is “this week’s marquee matchup” lets you anticipate attendance, booster‑club activity, and the angles editors will chase.

* **Participation Levels** – Almost every public high school fields a boys’ and a girls’ varsity team, plus junior varsity squads for the larger schools.  Private‑school and independent‑league teams (e.g., Beth Tfiloh, St. Peter & Paul) add depth and a chance for “underdog” story angles.  Because the talent pool is relatively compact, players often shift schools (transfers, re‑classing) and end up playing against former teammates, feeding narratives about loyalty, redemption, and local identity.

* **Seasonal Rhythms** –  
  * **Pre‑season (late October‑early November)** – Practices begin, coaches announce schedules, and the first “preview” pieces (tournament invites, roster cuts) are published.  
  * **Non‑conference play (mid‑Nov → early Dec)** – Teams test themselves against out‑of‑conference opponents; scoring trends and early injuries become useful data for trend pieces.  
  * **Conference play (mid‑Dec → late Feb)** – Rivalry games, home‑court advantages, and the battle for Bayside standings dominate coverage.  This is the period where game recaps, “how‑they‑got‑here” features, and weekly “standings‑at‑a‑glance” columns are most valuable.  
  * **Playoffs & Tournaments (early March → early Apr)** – The Bay‑Side conference tournament, regional brackets, and the state championship provide a cascade of high‑stakes games.  Expect overlapping schedules, travel to Salisbury, College Park, and other venues, and the need to prioritize which games get full‑court coverage versus quick‑turn recap wires.

## 1.  The Local Sports Landscape – What Matters Most

### Why Football Rules the Shore  
- **Community Tradition:** Friday night lights are a social calendar in towns from Easton to Cambridge. Homecoming games, “The Battle of the Bay” (Easton vs. Kent Island), and the annual **Colonel‑Richardson vs. North Caroline** rivalry draw packed bleachers, booster‑club booths, and local news coverage that can eclipse every other sport.  
- **Participation Levels:** Almost every public high school fields a varsity and a junior varsity squad. In many districts the JV team is a pipeline for the varsity, so you’ll get two games worth of story angles each week.  
- **Rivalries & Story Hooks:**  
  * **Easton vs. Kent Island** – a “big‑brother‑little‑brother” quarterback saga (the O’Connor brothers).  
  * **Cambridge‑South Dorchester (CSD) vs. Fort Hill** – the only 1A program with three straight state titles (2000‑2002) and a modern resurgence under Rob Burnham.  
  * **Queen Anne’s County (QA) vs. Colonel Richardson** – a clash of “gritty” versus “big‑play” philosophies.  
  * **Stephen Decatur vs. Huntingtown** – recent back‑to‑back 2A champions, a benchmark for any program aspiring to the state stage.  

### Seasonal Rhythm  
- **Late July – Early August:** Pre‑season camps and “scrimmage” reports. Focus on roster cuts, position battles, and the first look at the coaching staff’s new schemes.  
- **Early September – Mid‑November:** Regular‑season games (usually Fridays, sometimes Saturdays). The rhythm is **two games per week** for a handful of schools that share a field (e.g., Kent Island & Kent County), so expect double‑coverage deadlines.  
- **Late November – Early December:** Playoffs (regional, then state). Overlaps explode—football, basketball, and sometimes baseball share the same facilities. Your calendar will be a tangle of travel, rain‑outs, and “must‑see” games.  
- **Post‑Season (Early December):** Wrap‑up features, all‑conference selections, and recruiting‑season prep for seniors heading to college.
`````
