## 1.  The Local Sports Landscape – What Matters Most

### Why Football Rules the Shore  
- **Community Tradition:** Friday night lights are a social calendar in towns from Easton to Cambridge. Homecoming games, “The Battle of the Bay” (Easton vs. Kent Island), and the annual **Colonel‑Richardson vs. North Caroline** rivalry draw packed bleachers, booster‑club booths, and local news coverage that can eclipse every other sport.  
- **Participation Levels:** Almost every public high school fields a varsity and a junior varsity squad. In many districts the JV team is a pipeline for the varsity, so you’ll get two games worth of story angles each week.  
- **Rivalries & Story Hooks:**  
  * **Easton vs. Kent Island** – a “big‑brother‑little‑brother” quarterback saga (the O’Connor brothers).  
  * **Cambridge‑South Dorchester (CSD) vs. Fort Hill** – the only 1A program with three straight state titles (2000‑2002) and a modern resurgence under Rob Burnham.  
  * **Queen Anne’s County (QA) vs. Colonel Richardson** – a clash of “gritty” versus “big‑play” philosophies.  
  * **Stephen Decatur vs. Huntingtown** – recent back‑to‑back 2A champions, a benchmark for any program aspiring to the state stage.  

### Seasonal Rhythm  
- **Late July – Early August:** Pre‑season camps and “scrimmage” reports. Focus on roster cuts, position battles, and the first look at the coaching staff’s new schemes.  
- **Early September – Mid‑November:** Regular‑season games (usually Fridays, sometimes Saturdays). The rhythm is **two games per week** for a handful of schools that share a field (e.g., Kent Island & Kent County), so expect double‑coverage deadlines.  
- **Late November – Early December:** Playoffs (regional, then state). Overlaps explode—football, basketball, and sometimes baseball share the same facilities. Your calendar will be a tangle of travel, rain‑outs, and “must‑see” games.  
- **Post‑Season (Early December):** Wrap‑up features, all‑conference selections, and recruiting‑season prep for seniors heading to college.
